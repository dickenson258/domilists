Looking for a YouTube Kids Alternative? Four Ways to Show Your Child Only Videos You Picked

By a dad and indie developer · Updated 19 November 2026

If you want your child to see only the videos you chose, you have two straightforward options: switch YouTube Kids to its "Approve content yourself" setting, or build a hand-picked list in a separate tool such as Domi Lists, which is free and needs no account. Choose the first if your family already uses Google accounts and the YouTube Kids app; choose the second if you want a quick list you can share, without signing in.

Domi Lists is made by the author of this guide. We have tried to describe every option fairly.

YouTube says its systems work to exclude content not suitable for each age setting, and that not all videos have been manually reviewed. With any of these options, videos may show ads or platform controls.

Option 1: The YouTube Kids app

YouTube Kids is YouTube's app for children aged 12 and under. It can work in two quite different ways.

With an age setting

When you set it up, you pick Preschool (4 and under), Younger (5 to 8) or Older (9 to 12). The app then shows videos that YouTube's systems consider suitable for that age. It is quick to set up and gives your child plenty to explore. Your child chooses from what the app offers, so check in from time to time and block anything you do not like.

With "Approve content yourself"

This setting turns YouTube Kids into a hand-picked list. Your child can watch only the videos, channels and collections you have approved, and search is turned off. You need to sign in with a parent Google account. It is a good fit if you already use YouTube Kids and want the most control inside Google's own app. Approving content one item at a time takes a while at first.

Option 2: A supervised account on main YouTube

A supervised account is for children under 13 whose parents think they are ready for the main YouTube app. A parent manages it through Google and picks one of three content levels. It opens up far more videos than YouTube Kids, so it tends to suit older children more than toddlers and preschoolers.

Option 3: A playlist you make on YouTube

Many parents simply make a YouTube playlist of videos they like. It is free, plays in order and is easy to share. The playlist opens in the regular YouTube app or site, so your child can still search and open other videos. This works best when you are sitting with your child.

Option 4: A hand-picked watchlist in Domi Lists

Domi Lists is a free web app for parents to build and share video watchlists for their children. You paste in YouTube or Bilibili links, or start from a ready-made list, and save it on your device. No account is required. Your child watches in kids mode, which shows only the videos you saved, and the parent area is protected by a PIN you set. You can also set a daily watch-time limit.

Videos play in their original YouTube or Bilibili players, which may show ads or platform controls. YouTube and Bilibili work right away. Some other sites need an optional helper.

What to know before you choose it:

  • You pick every video, so there is some work at the start. Ready-made lists help.
  • The list is saved in the browser on that device. To use it on another device, share the list link and save it there, or turn on the optional family sync.
  • A video only plays if its owner allows it to be embedded and it is available in your country.

How to set up "Approve content yourself" in YouTube Kids
  1. Open the YouTube Kids app and go to Settings.
  2. Complete the parent check, then choose your child's profile and sign in with your parent account.
  3. Choose Edit settings, then Approve content yourself.
  4. Pick the videos, channels and collections you want to allow, then tap Done.

Steps follow YouTube's help page for the app. Menu names can change, so check YouTube's guide if yours look different.

Which one should you choose?

  • You already use Google accounts and the YouTube Kids app: try "Approve content yourself".
  • You want a list ready in a few minutes, with no account, or one you can send to grandparents or another family: try Domi Lists.
  • You will sit and watch together anyway: a YouTube playlist may be all you need.
  • Your child is older and ready for more of YouTube: a supervised account may fit.
  • Whatever you use, short sessions and watching together help most at this age.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good alternative to YouTube Kids for toddlers?

If you want your toddler to see only videos you chose, you can use YouTube Kids with "Approve content yourself", or a hand-picked watchlist such as Domi Lists, which is free and needs no account. Both let you decide every video.

Can I make YouTube show my child only videos I picked?

Yes. In YouTube Kids, the "Approve content yourself" setting limits your child to videos, channels and collections you approved and turns off search. A separate tool like Domi Lists does the same with a list you build from YouTube or Bilibili links.

Is Domi Lists free?

Yes. Domi Lists is free and does not require an account. Family sync is optional.

Do Domi Lists videos have ads?

Videos play in their original YouTube or Bilibili players, which may show ads or platform controls.

Does Domi Lists work on a tablet?

Yes. It runs in a web browser, so it works on phones, tablets and computers. The list is saved in that browser.

Can I share a list with another parent?

Yes. Share the list link. The other parent opens it, saves their own copy and can change it without affecting yours.
